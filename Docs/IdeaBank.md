# The Royal Executioner: Idea Bank

**Everything here is exploratory.** Nothing is committed, ranked, or required to fit the current design. Ideas are kept recognisable and deliberately unpolished. Add freely; do not delete darker ideas just because they are undeveloped.

---

## Crimes and absurd laws

- **Breaking the First Night tradition.** Someone is condemned for not honouring the First Night law. This is institutionalised sexual coercion: the world may be brutal, but the humour and the horror should aim at the oppressive institution and its officials, never trivialise the victims.
- **Grazing cows on the wrong grass.**
- **Wearing the royal colour.**
- **Contradictory royal decrees and outdated laws.** Two decrees that cannot both be obeyed; a law nobody has repealed since a long-dead king's grudge.

## Unusual prisoners and situations

- **An already-dead prisoner.** Arrived dead; the sentence still has to be carried out.
- **An immortal criminal** repeatedly returning for execution.
- **A vampire** requiring a specialised execution.
- **A ghost sentenced to hanging.**
- **A noble demanding a completely bloodless execution.**
- **A condemned engineer or blacksmith** who is the only person able to repair the execution equipment.
- **A judge who has since been condemned.**
- **A wizard who must be executed using their own magic.**
- **A prisoner whose sentence must be performed under bizarre timing or equipment restrictions.**

## Political intrigue and corruption

- **Rushed summary executions** demanded by the King or another powerful official.
- **A falsely accused prisoner** the executioner knows or strongly suspects is innocent.
- **The accuser orders silence.** Options: obey, accept a bribe, delay, seek intervention, secretly release the prisoner, fake an execution.
- **Ripple effects.** Saving or executing particular prisoners influences later events: a revolution, an assassination, a succession crisis, regime change.
- **Yesterday's judges and officials become tomorrow's prisoners.**
- **The executioner's own name appears on the execution list.**

## Bureaucracy

- Paperwork whose requirements conflict with the sentence itself.
- Administrative errors (wrong name, wrong date, wrong prisoner) with fatal consequences either way.
- Offices that exist only to approve other offices.
- Requesting clarification from a judge who cannot be reached or does not exist any more.
- Fees, stamps and seals for every stage of an execution, including the executioner's own dismissal.

## Execution methods and equipment

- Starting point: axe and chopping block.
- Equipment condition and preparation (sharpening, cleaning, replacing parts) affecting outcome and spectacle.
- Specialised methods for specialised prisoners (vampire, ghost, wizard, bloodless demand).
- Possibly more elaborate mechanical or magical apparatus later. Not committed to a contraption-builder, physics simulation or puzzle.

## Player action ideas

*Official:* review orders and records; schedule or delay; maintain equipment; manage money; request clarification or intervention; seek pardons; challenge administrative errors; handle special sentencing requirements.

*Unofficial:* accept bribes; manipulate records or schedules; conceal information; help prisoners escape; fake executions or substitute prisoners; cooperate with or defy factions.

*Things actions might touch:* money, royal favour, public sentiment, suspicion, relationships, equipment, later narrative events.

## Endings

- Surviving in royal service.
- Being executed.
- Becoming a victim of revolution.
- Escaping the kingdom.
- Helping change the regime.

## Dialogue and humour opportunities

- Officials who treat the absurd with complete administrative seriousness.
- Prisoners who are more concerned about procedure than their life.
- Euphemisms, titles and ceremonial formulas for ordinary violence.
- Black humour balanced with occasional genuinely unsettling moments, so that the horror lands.
- Crowd reactions and public commentary on the scaffold.
- The executioner as the only person who reads the whole paperwork.
